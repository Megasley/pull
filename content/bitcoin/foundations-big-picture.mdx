## Step 3 — Try it: send a payment

Now you drive. Pick a fee, send the payment, and watch the network, the mempool and the chain. Then try a different fee and compare.

<TxJourney />

Fees are paid in <Term def="The smallest unit of bitcoin. 1 BTC is 100,000,000 satoshis (sats).">satoshis</Term>. The rates in the simulator are in sat/vB: satoshis for each virtual byte of block space.

<Callout type="tip" title="Fee rate, not fee amount">
  Miners look at the fee **rate**, not the total fee. The size of a transaction depends
  on how many coins it spends, not on how much money it moves.
</Callout>

## Step 4 — Who does what

You met four parts of the system. Each one checks something different.

| Part        | What it does                                                    | What it checks                                        | Close-up lesson                                                                                                        |
| ----------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Wallet**  | Keeps your keys. Builds and signs transactions.                 | That you have enough coins to spend                   | [Keys & Addresses](/roadmaps/bitcoin/lessons/basics-keys), [Wallet Architecture](/roadmaps/bitcoin/lessons/dev-wallet) |
| **Node**    | Keeps every block. Passes transactions and blocks to its peers. | **Every** rule, for every transaction and every block | [P2P Network](/roadmaps/bitcoin/lessons/basics-network), [Bitcoin Core & RPC](/roadmaps/bitcoin/lessons/dev-core-rpc)  |
| **Mempool** | The waiting room inside each node                               | That a transaction is valid before it can wait        | [Mempool & Fees](/roadmaps/bitcoin/lessons/basics-mempool)                                                             |
| **Miner**   | Builds blocks and does proof of work                            | Its own block, before it spends energy on it          | [Mining & Consensus](/roadmaps/bitcoin/lessons/basics-mining)                                                          |

<Callout type="warning" title="A common mistake">
  Many people think miners make the rules. They do not. Every node checks every block.
  If a miner breaks a rule, nodes reject the block, and the miner loses its reward and
  the energy it spent.
</Callout>

<Check
  prompt="A miner makes a block that pays itself 1,000 extra bitcoin. What happens?"
  options={[
    "Nodes accept it, because miners decide the rules",
    "Nodes reject the block, because it breaks the rules",
    "Only Bob's wallet notices",
    "Nodes accept it but mark it as suspicious",
  ]}
  answer={1}
  hint="Who checks every block?"
  explain="Each node checks the block for itself. A block that breaks a rule is invalid, no matter how much work went into it."
/>

**Done when:** you can say what a node checks and why miners cannot change the rules.

## Step 5 — See it on the real network (lab)

Everything you just played with happens live on the real network. Look at it.

1. Open [mempool.space](https://mempool.space/). The blocks on the **left** are waiting blocks: what miners will probably mine next. Each shows a range of fee rates.
2. The blocks on the **right** are real, mined blocks. Note the latest block height.
3. Click a waiting block, then click one transaction in it. Copy its **txid** and note its **fee rate** in sat/vB.
4. Predict: will it go into the next block, or wait? Compare its fee rate with the range of the first waiting block.
5. Come back later. Search for your txid. Which block is it in, and how many confirmations does it have now?

**Done when:** your lab notes have the txid, your prediction with a reason, and the block it confirmed in.
